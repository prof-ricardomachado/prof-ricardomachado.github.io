Solução 3.1.4.10.1.

A forma rotacional do Teorema de Green garante que \(\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_D (\text{rot}\,\vec{F} \cdot \vec{k}) \, dA\text{.}\)
Calculamos a componente \(\vec{k}\) do rotacional bidimensional:
\begin{equation*} (\text{rot}\,\vec{F}) \cdot \vec{k} = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(y+x^2) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(x+y^2) = 2x - 2y \end{equation*}
Integrando sobre o quadrado \(D = [0,1] \times [0,1]\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} \iint_D (2x - 2y) \, dA \amp = \int_0^1 \int_0^1 (2x - 2y) \, dx \, dy\\ \amp = \int_0^1 \left[x^2 - 2xy\right]_{x=0}^{x=1} \, dy\\ \amp = \int_0^1 (1 - 2y) \, dy\\ \amp = \left[y - y^2\right]_0^1 = (1 - 1) - (0) = 0 \end{align*}
